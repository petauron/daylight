01 / DIGITAL HOUSEKEEPING

A backup is only useful if you can restore it

A useful backup answers three questions: what did you save, how recent is it, and how would you get it back? A short note beside the backup can be more valuable than another vaguely named folder.

Start with what you cannot replace. Work in progress may need frequent copies; a finished project may only need a few clearly marked versions. Keep important files in separate places so a single failed device cannot take every copy with it.

Occasionally choose a small, unimportant file and restore it. This simple exercise can reveal a missing permission, a broken path, or an incomplete archive. Confirm that the new copy works before clearing out an old one.

02 / UNDERSTANDING NETWORKS

Why a fast connection can still feel slow

Think of bandwidth as how much a road can carry, and latency as how long a journey takes. A wider road can move more cargo at once, but it does not make the destination any closer.

A large download often benefits from extra bandwidth. Opening a page, using a remote desktop, or sending a message involves many small exchanges, where each wait matters. Occasional delays and retransmissions can also make an otherwise quick connection feel inconsistent.

When comparing connections, keep the route and time of day as similar as possible. Look at delay, variation, and sustained transfer speed separately. A single number is useful, but it cannot describe the whole experience.

03 / EVERYDAY TOOLS

A website can simply be a place to read

Short articles, documentation, and personal notes do not always need a complex application. Clear headings, readable lines, and recognizable links can make a modest page easy to use.

Fewer remote fonts, scripts, and tracking services mean fewer things to wait for or depend on. Add an image when it helps explain an idea. Leave enough space for the words to be easy to follow.

Simple should still be thoughtful. Text should fit a small screen, links should work with a keyboard, and the page should remain comfortable in a dark room. These quiet details help a website stay useful long after it is published.
